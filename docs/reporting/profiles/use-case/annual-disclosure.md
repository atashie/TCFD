## What this reader needs decided

**What can be filed, and what has to be said about what cannot.** The reader is assembling a
disclosure with their organisation's name on it, and their exposure is that a statement in it
turns out to be unsupportable.

So the useful output is not a risk rating. It is:

1. Which requirements this evidence base actually satisfies, and to what standard.
2. Which it does not, stated in a form they can put in the filing themselves.
3. What they would need to obtain, from whom, to close each gap.

A gap they can disclose is worth more than a number they cannot defend. Frame every
limitation as disclosable text, not as an apology.

## Metrics that lead

- **Coverage first.** Which hazards were assessed, which were not, and what that means for
  what can be claimed. This is the section that protects the filer.
- Then exposure by site and horizon, in the framework's own vocabulary — acute and chronic,
  short/medium/long term — so it transfers into the filing without re-derivation.
- Then trend, because "is it getting worse" follows "how bad is it" in every review meeting.
- The Climate Score is an internal screening aid. **Keep it out of a filing** unless the
  reader specifically wants a portfolio headline and understands how it is composed.

## Known confounders

- **The filing deadline drives everything**, and deadline pressure is what turns a
  provisional figure into a published one. Anything not yet decided must be visibly not
  decided, not quietly defaulted.
- **The organisation's own definitions win.** Time horizons, materiality thresholds and
  reporting boundaries are theirs. Ours are placeholders and must be replaced, not argued
  with.
- **Another vendor's numbers may already exist** and may disagree with ours for legitimate
  reasons — different hazard definitions, resolutions and baselines. Make ours checkable
  rather than asserting primacy.
- **Assurance is coming.** Where a disclosure will be assured, every figure needs a traceable
  path back to a source. That is what the delivery's CSVs and manifest are for; point at
  them.
- **Boundaries.** Own operations, upstream, downstream — this assessment covers own sites
  only, and the framework asks about the value chain.

## Questions for the customer

- Which framework, which jurisdiction, and what is the deadline?
- Have you published definitions of short, medium and long term? What are they?
- What is your materiality threshold, and is it stated in financial or operational terms?
- Will this be assured, and by whom?
- What did reviewers challenge in your last filing?
- Which sites are in scope for the filing — is the reporting boundary the same as the site
  list you sent us?

## Do not claim

- Do not describe the assessment as satisfying a requirement it only partially addresses.
  Partial is a legitimate answer; overstated is not.
- Do not let a screening result be presented as an audit-ready metric without naming what
  would have to be true for it to be one.
- Do not soften or relocate the limitations section to make the document read more
  confidently. This reader's exposure is personal, and a confident report that fails under
  challenge is worse for them than a qualified one.
- Do not supply a figure for a requirement whose method has not been agreed. Supply the
  reason instead.
