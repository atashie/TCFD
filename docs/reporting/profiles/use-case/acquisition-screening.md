## What this reader needs decided

**Which candidates advance, and which get a closer look before they do.** That is a
comparative decision, and it is the use case our data is genuinely well suited to — every
site is measured the same way, on the same grid, against the same baseline, so the *ranking*
is far more trustworthy than any individual number.

Say that explicitly. It is the strongest honest claim available: this assessment is good at
ordering candidates and weak at pricing any one of them.

Two decision points, and they need different treatments:

- **Screen out.** A high bar, used rarely. Recommending exclusion on a screening result
  requires the exposure to be extreme, robust across scenarios, and driven by a hazard we
  actually cover well.
- **Escalate.** The common outcome. "This site warrants a site-specific assessment before
  committing, for this specific reason" is nearly always more defensible than a
  recommendation to walk away.

## Metrics that lead

- Rank order across candidates first — that is the answer to their question.
- Then, for the top candidates, the hazard driving each ranking, because two sites ranking
  alike for different reasons need different diligence.
- Then trend, which distinguishes a site that is already exposed from one that is becoming
  exposed. For an acquisition these have different implications: the first is priced in
  today, the second may not be.

## Known confounders

- **Coordinate precision decides the answer.** At this footprint, moving a site a quarter of
  a degree changed one measured value by 166%. A pin dropped on a town centre standing in
  for a tract can reverse a ranking. Confirm coordinates before any screening result is
  acted on — this is the single highest-value check in the whole use case.
- **Ranking is only valid within a hazard set.** Candidates mapped to different asset types
  carry different hazards and cannot be ranked against each other on the Climate Score.
- **Timing.** Screening happens under deal pressure, so the result will be used faster than
  it is read. Put the decisive qualification in the same sentence as the number.
- **Absence of flood is decisive here.** If candidates differ mainly in flood exposure — and
  for many land acquisitions they do — this assessment cannot separate them at all, and
  saying so is more useful than any ranking we can produce.

## Questions for the customer

- Are the coordinates tract centroids, office addresses, or surveyed boundaries?
- What is the shortlist, and what is the decision date?
- What would actually cause a candidate to be dropped, in their process?
- Is there an incumbent portfolio these candidates would join? Concentration matters more
  than the standalone result.
- What diligence already happens, so this slots into it rather than duplicating it.

## Do not claim

- Do not recommend acquiring or declining an asset. Rank, explain the driver, and identify
  what further work would resolve the question.
- Do not present a ranking as a valuation input.
- Do not rank candidates whose hazard sets differ without saying that the comparison is
  partial.
- Do not let a screening result stand on an unconfirmed coordinate.
