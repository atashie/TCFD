## Transmission channels

**Tropical cyclone dominates, and our coverage of it is the weakest in the assessment**
[jof-hurricane-wind-risk-forests]. That combination — highest materiality, thinnest data —
is the defining fact about any Gulf Coast site in this report and belongs near the top of
anything written about one.

Specifically, the cyclone layer here is:

- **wind only.** Storm surge, rainfall flooding and the compound coastal event are not
  covered by it or by any other layer.
- **a single impact model**, so its confidence interval carries no structural uncertainty.
- **the most heavily smoothed layer**, because raw storm tracks are one grid cell wide.
- **available at low and medium forcing only.** There is no high-forcing scenario, so any
  high-tier statement about a Gulf Coast portfolio is a statement about fire and drought.

For built assets, the loss channel is rarely the building alone: it is **business
interruption** through power, port, road and workforce disruption, all of which extend well
beyond the damaged structure and none of which the model addresses.

**Flooding is the largest silent gap.** Riverine, pluvial and coastal flood are all absent.
On this coastline that is not a minor omission — for a low-lying site it is likely the
dominant physical risk, and it is invisible in the results.

## Known confounders

- **Elevation, and distance from open water, matter more than the coordinate conveys.** A
  ~1° footprint spans the entire gradient from barrier island to inland.
- **Correlated loss.** A storm large enough to damage one Gulf asset damages the others.
  Site-level results treat them independently and understate portfolio exposure.
- **A coastal or offshore site may read as unassessed.** Extraction draws on land cells and
  a genuinely offshore site returns nothing at all. That is a masking outcome, not a low
  result, and must never be presented as one.
- **Insurance and building-code regimes** have changed substantially along this coast and
  may bind before any projected climate change does.

## Framing and vocabulary

"Hurricane" in prose; "tropical cyclone" only where it must match a data label. Name the
state and the county or parish. Distinguish coastal from inland explicitly — readers here
do, and the distinction carries most of the risk difference.

## Questions for the customer

- Elevation and distance from open water, per site.
- Storm history at the site, and how business interruption was handled.
- Whether the site is in a designated flood zone, and what flood cover exists.
- Single points of failure — one access road, one substation, one port.
- For offshore or near-shore assets: whether they are within our land-based modelled domain
  at all.

## Do not claim

- Do not describe a Gulf Coast site as fully assessed for storm risk. Wind only, one model,
  no high-forcing scenario, no surge, no flood.
- Do not present a high-tier result as the worst case for these sites; the dominant hazard
  is absent from that tier.
- Do not read an unassessed offshore site as low risk.
- Do not infer portfolio resilience from independent site results in a region where events
  are correlated.
