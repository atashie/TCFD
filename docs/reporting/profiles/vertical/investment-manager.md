## What this reader needs decided

An investment manager holds assets on behalf of somebody else, for a defined period, against
a stated mandate. Three consequences shape every report written for one:

- **The hold period is the horizon that matters**, and it is usually much shorter than the
  projection period. A fund with eight years left does not act on a 2090s result; it acts on
  the 2030s, and on what a buyer in 2034 will believe about the 2090s. Both matter, and they
  are different questions.
- **Exit value is a channel in its own right.** Climate risk reaches this reader partly
  through physical damage and partly through what a future buyer will pay once the same
  hazard information is universally available. The second often arrives first.
- **They report upward.** Whatever we produce will be re-presented to limited partners or a
  board, so it must survive being excerpted. Assume any single figure may be lifted out of
  context, and make each one carry its own qualification.

## Framing and vocabulary

Portfolio, holding, tract or site, hold period, mandate, LP, diligence, underwriting, exit.
Concentration matters more than any single asset: a portfolio with one severely exposed
holding and nine unexposed ones is a different proposition from ten moderately exposed ones,
and the report should answer that question directly rather than leaving it to be inferred
from a table.

## Metrics that lead

- Distribution across the portfolio before any individual site.
- Concentration: how much of the portfolio, and how much of the value, sits in the worst
  band.
- Divergence between scenarios at the hold-period horizon — a spread that is narrow within
  the hold period and wide beyond it is itself the finding.

## Known confounders

- **Correlated loss is invisible here.** Site-level exposure treats each holding
  independently, but a regional event hits several at once. A portfolio concentrated in one
  region is more exposed than the sum of its site results implies, and our data cannot
  quantify that.
- **Unequal asset sizes.** Counts weight a small holding equally with a large one. Without
  asset values every portfolio statistic in the report is by count, and that should be
  stated wherever a percentage appears.
- **Uneven hazard coverage across the portfolio.** Different asset types are mapped to
  different hazard sets, so a cross-asset comparison is not always like-for-like.

## Questions for the customer

- Fund term and remaining hold period, per holding if they differ.
- Whether the mandate carries climate or sustainability commitments that constrain action.
- What their LPs already ask for, and in what format.
- Whether asset carrying values can be shared — this is what unlocks the monetary half of
  the disclosure metrics.
- Whether acquisitions are screened for climate today, and by whom.

## Do not claim

- Do not imply a valuation impact. We have no financial model.
- Do not present portfolio percentages as value-weighted when they are count-weighted.
- Do not suggest the portfolio is diversified against climate risk on the basis of
  site-level results — that requires correlation we do not model.
