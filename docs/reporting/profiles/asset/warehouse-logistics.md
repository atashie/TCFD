## Transmission channels

A warehouse is a **large, low, wide, cheaply-clad structure whose value is mostly in what it
enables rather than what it is**. Two channels, and the second is usually larger:

- **Physical damage** — roof and cladding failure under wind, water damage to stock. The
  structure is replaceable; the inventory and the fit-out often are not insured to the same
  standard.
- **Interruption of function** — road access, power, workforce availability, and the fate of
  the sites this one serves. A warehouse that is undamaged but unreachable is out of service,
  and a distribution network fails at its weakest node rather than its average one.

Our assessment addresses the first channel partially and the second not at all.

**Drought is deliberately excluded** from the catalog mapping for this asset type. Water is
not a process input to a warehouse, and reporting a hazard with no transmission channel to
the asset is worse than reporting nothing — it pads the assessment and invites the reader to
treat the whole document as generic.

## Metrics that lead

- Wind exposure where the region profile says storms reach the site.
- Fire exposure where the surrounding landscape burns — noting that for an isolated
  industrial structure this is a weaker channel than for vegetated land.
- Trend, because a built asset has a defined economic life and a refurbishment cycle that a
  changing exposure interacts with.

## Known confounders

- **Network position matters more than site exposure.** A modestly exposed site that is the
  single node serving a region carries more risk than a highly exposed one with redundancy.
  Nothing in this assessment sees network structure.
- **Design standard is decisive and invisible.** Two warehouses at the same coordinates built
  to different wind codes have very different outcomes.
- **Interruption is not damage.** Most loss for this asset class arrives through downtime,
  and our layers measure hazard occurrence rather than service continuity.
- **Flooding is the classic warehouse hazard and is not covered at all.** Low-lying, flat,
  large-footprint sites near transport corridors are precisely the flood-exposed archetype.

## Framing and vocabulary

Facility, site, throughput, downtime, business interruption, single point of failure. Talk
about days of disruption rather than degrees of hazard — that is the unit the operator plans
in.

## Questions for the customer

- What this facility serves, and what happens to the network if it stops.
- Design standard and construction date, particularly the roof and cladding specification.
- Flood zone status and stormwater arrangements.
- Access redundancy — one road or several, and grid supply arrangements.
- Whether inventory value or facility value is the exposure that matters here.

## Do not claim

- Do not present hazard exposure as expected downtime; nothing here models service
  continuity.
- Do not imply the assessment covers flooding, which for this asset class is often the
  dominant risk.
- Do not add drought to the assessment for a warehouse to make coverage look fuller.
- Do not compare a warehouse and a timber holding on the Climate Score. They carry different
  hazard sets and the comparison is not like-for-like.
